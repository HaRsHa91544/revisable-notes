# Lecture 11
## Air pollution: The presence of harmful substances in air at higher concentrations or durations which effects living beings.

## Air pollutant: It is not a part of natural air composition or is present at a higher concentration than its natural level.

## Two types of pollution:
1. Outdoor - Surroundings
2. Indoor - Household

## Sources of air pollution
1. Point source: A single identifiable source at location.
2. Area source: Many small sources whose combined emmissions can be significant.
3. Mobile source: Emmissions from sources that move one place to another.

- Point source and Area source -> Stationary sources
- Mobile source -> Line sources

## Classification of air pollutants based on physical form:
1. Gaseous pollutants: CO, SO2, NO2.
2. Particulate matter: Tiny solid particles or liquid droplets in air.

## Primary pollutants: Emmitted directly from the source into air. Ex: CO, NO2, SO2, etc.
## Secondary pollutants: Formed in atmosphere when primary pollutants reacts with other substances. Ex: Ground-level Ozone, peroxyacetyl nitrates (PAN).

## Stratospheric ozone: Beneficial because it shields earth from UV rays.
## Ground-level ozone: Harmful pollutant formed by atmospheric chemical reactions.

## Particulate Matter:
1. PM10 - size < 10um -> irritate nose and upper respiratory tract. (Dange)
2. PM2.5 - size < 2.5um -> Travel deep into lungs. (More danger)
3. PM0.1 - size < 0.1um -> Travel into other organs beyond respiratory. (Most danger)
